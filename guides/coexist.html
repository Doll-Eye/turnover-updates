TurnOver and VoiceOver

TurnOver is a second screen reader for the Mac, a choice beside VoiceOver. One reader speaks at a time, as on every other computer: while TurnOver is on, it reads everything and VoiceOver is off; turn TurnOver off and VoiceOver is back. You choose which you use, and how often you switch.

Keeping some apps with VoiceOver

If VoiceOver reads an app better for you, it can keep that app while TurnOver reads the rest. In the app, press Command Control Shift T: VoiceOver takes it over, and TurnOver remembers your choice next time. The same key gives it back to TurnOver. The first press turns on Readers per app, in Settings, Screen reader behaviour, where you can turn it off again.

The list of VoiceOver's apps is under Tools in the TurnOver menu, where you can also remove one. With Readers per app on you can also choose a mode: TurnOver everywhere except your VoiceOver apps (the usual one), TurnOver only in its own apps, or VoiceOver everywhere.

The swap

Settings, Screen reader behaviour, TurnOver and VoiceOver swap is on unless you turn it off. On, one reader comes on as the other goes off. Off, each has its own switch and neither turns the other on or off, which is for VoiceOver reading inside a virtual machine such as Parallels. Either way, while VoiceOver is on, TurnOver is quiet.

CoExist (experimental)

CoExist is an older way of working, kept as an experiment and off unless you turn it on (Settings, Screen reader behaviour, Experimental). With it, VoiceOver keeps running beside TurnOver and is silenced at the sound level wherever TurnOver reads; VoiceOver is heard in the apps you give it, in password fields and in macOS's own prompts. TurnOver's own braille is off while it's on, as VoiceOver keeps the display. Changing it restarts TurnOver.

It can leave a Mac silent if macOS asks for audio permission while VoiceOver is muted, and a macOS or VoiceOver update could change how it behaves. Please report anything odd.
