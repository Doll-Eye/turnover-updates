Check it yourself

You don't have to take our word for it. Install a firewall that shows every connection, such as LuLu (free, from objective-see.org) or Little Snitch, and use TurnOver as usual. You will see TurnOver contact its update site, and nothing else.

What TurnOver keeps

The log is plain text: you are welcome to open it and read it.

A feedback report holds these logs. "Send a report with what TurnOver said" also includes what TurnOver said aloud in the last 15 minutes, which can be the text of an email or page you were reading; "Send a report without what TurnOver said" leaves it out. Either way the report is a file on your Desktop: you decide whether to attach it, and since GitHub reports are public, choose the one without what TurnOver said if anything private was on screen.

Who can talk to TurnOver

TurnOver's own helpers (its Settings window, the Command Control Period helper, the plugin tool, Windy) reach it through a private channel in TurnOver's own folder. Before anything is read, macOS checks that the program on the other end is signed by TurnOver's developer; any other program is turned away and the refusal is logged. A copy you build yourself without a developer certificate accepts only the programs inside its own app. Like any app's, TurnOver's settings file can be changed by programs running as you; a script you give a key to asks you before it first runs.

Apps that speak through TurnOver

An app can ask TurnOver to speak for it (a game, for example), through a port on this Mac only. TurnOver works out for itself which program is asking (from the Mac's own records, not from what the app says), and the first time asks you, by name, whether you trust it. An app you allow is heard only while it is the app in front, and it can do nothing but speak: no settings, no keys. Settings, Apps that speak through TurnOver, lists them and changes your answer.
