How to use TurnOver

TurnOver is a screen reader for the Mac that works alongside VoiceOver. VoiceOver stays on; TurnOver reads every app with its own voice and the keys you already know, and VoiceOver handles passwords and macOS's own security prompts. This page is the short tour. Each section links to the guide that goes into detail.

Three things to remember

  1. Command Control Comma is the way in to everything TurnOver: press it, hear "TurnOver", then a letter. M is the TurnOver menu, S the Settings window, K the keyboard style, H these guides. Any other key reads the list.
  2. Command Control Period turns TurnOver off and on, from anywhere. Off, VoiceOver reads as usual.
  3. Command Control Shift T gives the app in front to VoiceOver, or back to TurnOver, and remembers it.

Everything else is the keys of the screen reader you already know. See The TurnOver layer.

Reading

Arrow through text, documents, web pages and messages; say all reads to the end and stops where you were. In a web page or a message, single letters jump by heading, link, button or form field, and Return or Space presses. See Reading and say all.

The apps

TurnOver reads every app. For the ones people use most, it adds keys and reading of its own: Mail, Messages and WhatsApp, Word and Excel, Safari, Music, Podcasts, Calendar, Pages, Numbers, Keynote, Terminal, Steam and more. Each app's own keys are listed at the end of the key pages, and described in The apps. An app that VoiceOver does better can stay VoiceOver's: Command Control Shift T.
